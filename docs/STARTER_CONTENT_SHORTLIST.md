Researched and user-approved 2026-09-24. All four entries are now included in
the local Explore catalog, with creator attribution and verified duration.
The isolated preview simulates subtitle readiness. Production readiness must
come from the real transcript cache, never from the editorial catalog.

## Direction

Keep routine suggestions focused: **Learn English**, **Learn French**, and
**Choose another topic**. Broaden the things people watch, rather than adding
unsupported goal categories. Culture, psychology, science and music can be
content topics; spoken language is a separate property, not the topic itself.

A starter should have an immediate question/hook, be evergreen, need little
prior knowledge, and be short enough for a first-session finish. These are
editorial judgments, not claims that all four suit every learner. No certified
language levels or personalization are implied.

Titles/creators/descriptions were screened on the creators' YouTube pages.
Durations, original-language codes and caption-track availability were checked
directly using local yt-dlp metadata extraction, without cookies, credentials,
video downloads, application imports or database access. A non-automatic track
is not proof of an error-free human transcript.

This is a researched shortlist, not a full editorial watch-through. In particular,
Easy French's description advertises its own membership and other services, so
review in-video promotional interruptions as part of the first-session fit check.
YouTube metadata alone does not establish reliable Xaana playback, subtitle
fetch success, cue quality or correct cache persistence. No production content
has been marked subtitle-ready based on this research.
